Dot Maps.

A driving test for AI helpers.

You wouldn't hand a stranger your car keys without a driving test. Dot Maps is that test for AI — before an AI helper touches a real job, it drives the course while everything gets recorded. Pass or fail is decided by math, not vibes, and you get the certificate and the tape.

Why it works

Same course, every time

The test never bends to make the AI look good. The course is frozen before the first attempt — so a pass means something.

Watched the whole way

Every attempt is recorded. You don't have to trust anyone's word — you can watch the tape yourself.

Pass/fail is math

The pass line is set before the test starts, and fails get published too. No grading on a curve, no hiding bad runs.

What a "map" actually is

A Dot Map is a predicate map: your task broken into checks a machine can verify pass/fail with no judgment calls — booking.time_slot_open, email.confirmation_sent. Steps that can be checked mechanically are typed deterministic; steps that need interpretation are typed ambiguous and flagged back to you to clarify or assign a verifier. That routing is the translation layer: it doesn't just build the test, it teaches you to specify.

The research claim (weak-model-as-instrument)

The examiner is deliberately a weak, frozen model. The literature improves weak models; Dot Maps inverts the direction of measurement — the weak model is the fixed instrument, and its convergence rate grades the map. A map only a genius can follow is a bad map. If the weakest driver can pass your course reliably, the course is well-built.

Statistics: pre-registered threshold θ, Wilson score intervals, certification when the interval's lower bound clears θ, published failure when the upper bound falls below it. Known confound, stated up front: convergence may partly measure map legibility to that one model rather than intrinsic quality — mitigated by running a grid of 3+ weak models × 3+ map sources and checking that map rankings stay stable.

How agents use this as a sandbox upgrade

Three surfaces. (1) The map bundle — a dotmap.json the agent reads before touching the workflow: predicates, frozen rules, certificate status, plus a capabilities manifest declaring required tools/MCP servers/skills. (2) The verifier endpoint — an MCP-shaped tool where agents submit traversals and receive graded verdicts; agents cannot self-grade. (3) The lifecycle — admit → traverse → certify → promote/demote: certified maps become trusted rungs other agents build on, and predicate drift demotes a map and everything stacked on it.

Where this goes: graphs, swarms, Forge

Certified maps compose: one map's exit predicates feed another's entry predicates, so trust propagates through a graph the way types propagate through a program. In a swarm, no central boss is needed — agents deposit traversals, convergent traversals cluster, and clusters that clear θ crystallize into certified maps (θ is the crystallization criterion). Forge is the economy on top: a network where certified maps are the unit of trusted, tradeable work.
