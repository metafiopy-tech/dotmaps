# The 12× Finding

If the efficiency claim died, what survived? Something better than a
discount: a real difference in how far a colony reaches once repeating
itself is off the table.

In the same final, cleanest trial where the cost claim was killed, the
colony that already knew things proposed **63 new, boundary-testing
claims about the world it hadn't seen before** — mutation rules, edge
cases, the genuinely unproven stuff. A colony starting from nothing, on
the same task, under the same budget, proposed **5**. Twelve times the
reach, measured, not estimated, in the one trial built cleanly enough to
trust the number.

And it wasn't a fluke of one run: across three separate trials, the
already-equipped side held onto deeper reach in twelve of thirteen
comparisons. One instrument, repeated, agreeing with itself.

**Why this makes sense once you see it:** every attempt an agent doesn't
have to spend re-confirming something it already knows is an attempt
freed up to go try something it doesn't. Nothing in that trade makes the
*next* fact cheaper to learn — the "What Died" page next door explains
why not — but it does mean more of a fixed budget goes toward ground
nobody's covered yet, instead of ground already settled. That's not an
efficiency instrument. It's a reach instrument, and it's the surviving,
publishable half of the original bet.

**Say it exactly right:** this is a measured campaign result, not a law
of nature yet. It's a real number from a real, cleanly-run trial, repeated
across three trials by the same team, with the same instrument — and it
hasn't been independently rerun by anyone outside this project. "12x
deeper" should be read as "what we found, so far, running our own
protocol" until an outside group reruns it and it holds. That's not a
hedge to bury the finding — it's the same standard the killed efficiency
claim got held to, and it's the reason that finding is trustworthy: this
project reports what it measured, not what it hoped for.
