# The Laws

Rules the team arrived at by getting burned, not by theorizing in
advance. Each one has a run that proves why it exists.

**Mechanical gates beat informational surfacing.** Telling an agent "here
are the five things left to do" as plain text got it right about half
the time (5 of 10 tries). Making the same five things a hard, structural
requirement — you cannot proceed until this specific thing is true —
got it right every time (36 of 36). A rule an agent can read and ignore
mid-task isn't a rule. A rule it structurally cannot get past is.
*(receipt: the human-flight log, flights 3–4.)*

**A check that cannot fail is not a check.** Before anything gets
believed, it has to be tested two ways: does it hold on a clean copy of
the world, and does it correctly fall apart on a deliberately broken
one? A claim that says yes to both copies is worthless no matter how
confident it sounds, and it gets thrown out at the gate, every time,
before it can ever be trusted. *(receipt: the confirm/discriminate gate,
exercised on every run.)*

**Do the work, then check the work — never both at once.** Asking an
agent to "verify" a task that was never actually performed doesn't make
the task happen; it just makes the agent very confident about nothing.
Execution and verification are two separate phases now, in that order,
with a hard mechanical stop between them if the first one didn't
actually finish. *(receipt: the do-then-verify build, landed after the
finding that caused it.)*

**Never touch a certified fact's own words on a routine read.** Using a
certified skill updates *when it was last used* and *how fresh it is* —
never the fact itself or the check that proves it. Every single use is
self-checked, byte for byte, to make sure nothing snuck in a silent
edit. *(receipt: the reconsolidation module's own safety assertion, run
on every certified card, every time.)*

**Silence is health.** She only reacts to what's on the record — never
polls, never nags, never narrates a wall of green checkmarks. One card:
either "nothing needs you," or a real decision with real options. A
repeat-check that comes back clean adds nothing new to the story, on
purpose — the story is for changes, not for reassurance. *(receipt: her
one-card status, and the diary's own changes-only rule.)*
